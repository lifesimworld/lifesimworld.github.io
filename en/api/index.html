LifeSim Engine · the life simulation behind LifeSim

LifeSim Engine API

The LifeSim Engine is the life simulation behind LifeSim, where you are born as one of the babies arriving on Earth right now, by the real share of births in 172 countries and territories, and live that one life. It connects figures from the UN, WHO and the World Bank into one model of how lives go. This API lends you that world: you cannot change it, but inside it you may do almost anything. Begin a life anywhere and in any year the world has, with any condition of birth it has; decide what the person decides; meet people of any age alive today; stop anywhere and live on. For stories, games and simulations of your own, apps, teaching and research, with a free key.

Everything here is simulated. The lives follow real statistics, but they are not the real world, real people, records or forecasts. Say so wherever you show them, above all in research.

  2. 2Add a custom connector

    https://lifesimworld.github.io/mcp
  3. 3Paste the key once

    Press ‘Connect’ and a lifesimworld.github.io page opens. Paste your key there once and you are done. The key never goes to the AI app.

Then just ask: ‘Live three generations, starting with a grandmother born in Korea in 1950’, or ‘Compare children in India in 1960 and today’. Each call uses your key’s units. The tools, what stays out and the protocol are below.

Answers are JSON (UTF-8) and can be called from a web page (CORS) to try the engine with your key typed in; in an app, call it from your server (see Keys). Each has a meta block (the API version, the engine's version, simulated: what the lives are and are not, your plan, links to this page and the credit line) and, on engine calls, usage. An AI tool can read the whole API from /api/v1/openapi.json.

From an AI app (MCP)

Add the engine as a connector in an AI app that speaks MCP (the Model Context Protocol), such as Claude: add a custom connector with the address https://lifesimworld.github.io/mcp. When you connect, our page opens and asks for your API key once; the app then holds its own tokens for that key (we keep only their hashes, the key is not kept, and the app never sees it). Ask in your own words, and the app calls the engine for you.

toolwhat
live_a_life/life: one to three lives (country, year or future, sex, fifth, place, record, seed, lang)
compare_lives/simulate: an outlook, up to 4 countries and 6 worlds side by side (country, years, sex, n, at, by, fifth, place, seed, lang)
meet_person/person: someone alive now, or someone looked for (country, age, sex, job, education, partnered, children, record, seed, lang)

Each tool call is an engine call on your key: the same units, limits and terms. The tools take the common parameters; the rest (who a person is, the lines that need care, /live, whose tokens are too large for a chat) stays in the HTTP API. Turning the key off, or writing for a new one, ends the app's connections: connect again with the new key.

For those building clients: Streamable HTTP without sessions (each POST one JSON-RPC message, answered as JSON; no stream); OAuth 2.1 with PKCE (S256) for public clients, Dynamic Client Registration (/oauth/register) and Client ID Metadata Documents; discovery at /.well-known/oauth-protected-resource/mcp and /.well-known/oauth-authorization-server; access tokens last an hour, refresh tokens 90 days and are replaced at each use.

Use it in a project

  • NPCs and virtual worlds: people alive now (/person) as your townspeople, with their work, family and the history they lived through; one /live call moves 10 of them a year on.
  • Apps and interactive stories: /live sends each decision with the odds the world gives it. You draw the screen; the person chooses.
  • AI evaluation and research: test an AI on people drawn by where people live, or compare lives under different conditions. Say that the results come from a simulation.
  • Classes and history: be born in any year from 1950; each year brings the country as it was and the history that reached people.
  • Every call and condition is below; AI tools can read /api/v1/openapi.json.

Without coding, with an AI

You do not need to write code. Ask the AI chat you use to make you a small page that uses the engine, then open it in your browser: a good way to learn how to build things with an AI. This guide is in every edition's language (the links at the top).

  1. Get a key:It starts with k8b_.
  2. Ask an AI: copy the text below into the AI chat you use. Do not give the AI your key.
  3. Open the file: download the HTML file the AI makes and open it in your browser. A preview inside the chat may block the connection.
  4. Experiment: paste your key in the box at the top, pick two countries and compare. If something goes wrong, paste the error on the screen into the AI chat and it will fix it.

Endpoints

The engine, with a key:

GETwhat
/api/v1/lifelives, each lived from birth to the end (or to the age you stop it): where and when it began, who the person is, school, work, partner, children, moves, how and where it ended, and if you like each year in numbers, each decision met, the record
/api/v1/simulatean outlook for the conditions you choose, over many lives at once: lifespan, survival, causes of death, at 40 schooling, work and income; partners, children, moves, what the lives met; grouped (by) and at the ages you ask (at); up to 4 countries and 6 worlds side by side in one call
/api/v1/lineagea family line, one family: a life, then the life of its eldest child who grew up, with the family that life gave it, for 2 to 5 generations
/api/v1/personpeople alive now: someone of any age in a country today, or someone you look for (a nurse in Korea, a grandmother in Peru), where they stand, with a token to live on from there
/api/v1/livePOST: a life lived from outside, a step at a time: you make each decision, set the focus of the year, spend from the sheet; the life's state travels as a sealed token

Catalogs, without a key: the codes the engine's answers use and their names, so you can show them in your language.

/api/v1this list
/api/v1/countriesthe country codes a life uses, with names in 13 languages and the region
/api/v1/causesthe cause of death codes, with names in 13 languages
/api/v1/jobsthe job codes, with names in 13 languages and the sector

The world lent

The world stays as it is: its odds, its events and its tables are the engine's, and no call changes them. Inside it you choose: a condition of birth you ask for (fifth, place, faith, group, disability, orientation, caste, gender) is found among the world's own births, at their real shares, never made up; a combination the world does not have, or has too rarely, is refused (400). What the person decides is yours: a policy, picks by card, or each decision yourself with /live. What happens to them is the world's.

Lives on request

The engine endpoints take these parameters, all optional:

parameterwhat
countryISO 3166-1 alpha-3 code. Leave it out and the engine draws the country by the share of births, as LifeSim does (/person: by the number of people living there). On simulate, up to 4 codes, comma-separated (KOR,NGA): one run for each.
yearssimulate: up to 6 worlds to compare in one call, comma-separated: birth years 1950 to 2025, today, future (e.g. 1960,1990,today,future): one run for each, and for each country asked; at most 8 runs a call. Not with year or future.
langthe language of the record, the decisions, the events of /live and the names in them: en (the default), ko, ja, zh (Traditional, Taiwan usage), vi, fr, th, es, de, pt (Brazil), ru, tr, id, it, ar, fa, uk, pl, as LifeSim tells them. Codes and field names stay as they are. A /live life keeps the language it began in.
sexfemale or male. Leave it out for the draw (life, person) or for both (simulate).
yeara past birth year, 1950 to 2025: each year of the life is its country as it was then, with the wars, famines and crises of that year, and the projections after 2026. Leave out year and future for today's world, held as it is for the whole life.
future1: born today into the coming world: each year is the country as projected (UN WPP 2024 medium variant), with shocks at the rates history shows, AI changing work, ageing and a warming climate. Not with year.
fifth, placethe family at birth: fifth 1 to 5, its place among the country's income fifths; place town or village.
faith, group, disability, orientationwho the person is: faith christian, muslim, hindu, buddhist, jewish, none, folk, sikh, other; group an id of /api/v1/groups or majority; disability movement, hearing, sight (born with it), autism, intellectual, or none; orientation straight, gay_lesbian, bisexual, asexual (drawn at 12, as the simulation does; where the law punishes it, the life keeps it hidden: kept_hidden).
castean id of the castes in /api/v1/groups: the categories the state itself uses, in India (scheduled_caste, obc, general, dalit_not_scheduled; the Scheduled Tribes are group=in_adiv) and Nepal (dalit, khas_arya, janajati, madhesi), never a rank; the country follows. What a life meets is what the data has: being treated worse, not who one is, with the law and the reserved places as the way forward.
genderonly with sensitive=1 (a trans life's story is one LifeSim keeps on the device): trans_woman, trans_man, non_binary or cisgender, drawn at 12 and only where a national count exists (Canada, the UK, the US: elsewhere the engine has none to draw, which does not mean there are none). sex stays the sex registered at birth. A trans man or woman is called by the name they chose (lines with the name they were given are left out), and whom they love is said by the gender they live as.
policy, pickwhat the person decides when you do not: policy auto (what most people choose; the default) or ambitious; pick card:choice pairs, e.g. college:uni,emigrate:go (the card ids and choice keys show in decisions).
to, trace, decisionslife: to an age where the life stops, alive (its state in now); trace=1 each year in numbers; decisions=1 each decision met, its choices and the one made.
by, atsimulate: by sex, fifth, place, faith, group, disability, orientation, caste or gender (gender with sensitive=1) (the lives grouped by it, each group as many as the world gives); at up to 6 ages (e.g. 20,40,60) for what the lives held then.
nhow many: for life and person 1 to 5 on the free plan (default 1); for simulate, lives of each sex (default 30), up to the plan's life units a call (60 on the free plan).
seeda whole number from 0 to 4294967295. The same seed on the same engine version and in the same language gives the same lives; n lives use seed, seed + 1, ... (simulate's runs: seed + 2 for each run after the first). Leave it out for new lives each time (each life says the seed it had). In another language the same seed begins the same life, but the small moments the engine picks follow its own sentences, so the lives may part after some years.
enginelife and simulate: a research edition, YYYY-MM (the ones kept are editions in /api/v1). Each May and November the engine as it is then is frozen and kept for a year, so a finding can be run again by anyone with the same calls and seeds; the answer says its edition and the line to cite. The record in English or Korean (lang). Leave it out for the current engine, which changes almost every day.
generations, trieslineage: generations 2 to 5 (default 3); tries 1 to 5 (default 3), how many times a generation that must go on is lived again when it had no child who grew up. year is the first generation's (default 1950).
recordlife, person and lineage, 1: the year-by-year record as the simulation tells it, in English (or the language of lang), as [age, text] or [age, text, source] when the line carries a real figure; brief: only the lines that carry the life (what happened, losses, milestones, history, hard years, the end), as [age, text], about a third of the whole.
sensitive, suicidesensitive=1: the record's lines that LifeSim keeps on the reader's own device (drug dependence, sexual violence, violence at home, assisted dying and others) come too, each with its help line. suicide=1: a suicide is named, as a cause and in the record, with a help line beside it. Without them those lines are left out and a suicide's cause is empty. Ask for them only when your work needs them, and present them with care (see the terms).

With several countries (country=KOR,NGA) or worlds (years=1960,1990,today,future), or both, the answer has runs: one for each country and world, each {country, year, world, female, male} with the figures above, in the order asked (each country with each world). /api/v1/simulate?country=KOR,NGA&n=30 is the comparison of two countries in one call; /api/v1/simulate?country=IND&years=1960,1990,today&at=10 shows how childhood changed (electricity_at_home at 10 among them). Each run counts its own units.

Lives are lived at the simulation's own level, the one LifeSim itself uses (15 and over): hard roads come at their real shares (prison, dependence, violence), and the lines that need most care leave only when you ask (sensitive, suicide). Lives are simulated: they follow the real odds, so most are ordinary, and none is a real person's.

People alive now

/api/v1/person meets someone living today: born age years ago (0 to 76; left out, drawn from the people living there by age) and lived up to today, with the policy and picks you give. The answer is where they stand (state: name, age, country, place, job, income, net worth, health, happiness, knowledge, bonds, partner, children, parents, who they are; and the family as it stands: household, who lives under the same roof and how many in all, siblings with their ages and who is still at home, the partner's work and faith, the parents' work, each child at home or not, abroad, married, at work) and their family_at_birth; ask for trace, decisions or record for the years behind them, and token=1 for a token to live on with /live.

Looking for someone: job (an id of /api/v1/jobs, or agriculture, industry, services), education (at least), partnered, children (at least), abroad, working. Each try lives one person to that age, so tries (default 10 with a search, at most 200) says how many may be met, and each counts; tries_used says how many were. Someone the world rarely has may not be found: allow more tries, or ask for less.

Generations

/api/v1/lineage?country=KOR&year=1950&generations=4 lives a family line, one family: the first life from its birth year to its end, then the life of its eldest child who grew up in it (living at its end, or past fifteen when it died), then that child's, and so on. Each child is born in the year the parent had it, in the country and the town or village where the parent lived then, into the income fifth the household had then, of the family's faith and group (found among the world's own births, never made), and into the family the parent's life gave it: its name, the parents' ages, whether the other parent was there and a parting, a mother's new partner, its brothers and sisters as they came, the years its parents and the parent's own parents died, and life at least to the age the parent's record follows it to. The parent's record leaves the child's years from fifteen to the child's own generation. A family line is the people who had children: a generation that must go on and had no child who grew up is lived again (tries, each counted in the work it does), and when none of the tries had one the line stops there (ended: no_children). A child born after today is born into the coming world, from today (future). The answer has generations, each a life as /life gives it with generation and born_to (the generation before and its age then), and tries_used; record, trace, decisions, lang, policy and pick apply to every generation, the conditions of birth to the first.

A life lived from outside

POST /api/v1/live with a JSON body. To begin: {} or the conditions (country, sex, year, future, age to begin as someone alive now, fifth, place, faith, group, disability, orientation, caste, gender, policy, pick, sensitive, suicide, seed). The life runs to its first decision. Then, each step: {"token": "...", "choose": "key"}, and as you like "focus": "key" (the year's focus), "buy": ["id"] (from the spending sheet, bought after the step's choice, before the life goes on), "until": "decision" (the default: on to the next one), "year" (to the next birthday, stopping at a decision), or an age (decisions on the way made by the policy and picks; at most 25 years a call).

To look without living on (after a reload, say): {"token": "...", "view": true} gives where the life stands and what waits, counts no units and gives the same token back.

Several lives at once (an app with many people living): {"steps": [{"token": "...", "choose": "key"}, {"token": "...", "view": true}]}, up to 10, gives results in the same order, each the answer below or its own error (error, code); each step counts one unit (a view none), and the call counts once against the calls a minute.

Each answer has state (as in /person), events (the lines of the years since the last step, [age, text]), decision (id, question, about, fact, choices with key, label, note; null when none waits), focus (now and the options), sheet (what can be spent on now, also while a decision waits: id, name, does, price_ppp, can, why), ended and death at the end, and a new token. The token holds the whole life, sealed: we keep nothing, it cannot be read or changed, and it works for 90 days. Keep it to come back later, or send the same token again with another choice to see the other road: the world's dice go on the same way from there.

With care

Some lines LifeSim keeps on the device of the person living the life. Here they leave only when you ask: sensitive=1 for drug dependence, sexual violence (never under 15), violence at home, assisted dying, a trans life's story (and gender) and the others; suicide=1 for a suicide, as a cause and in the record, always with a help line beside it. The simulation tells them without method, place or detail, from the person's own side, with help-seeking and recovery as the usual course; keep it that way where you show them. Caste and a trans life are told the same way: from the person's own side, gaps only as the data has them, being treated worse and never who one is as what costs. The engine's own tables (its life tables, cause weights and the figures it is built on) are not served.

Limits

The engine is counted in life units: one life is one unit, and a life born in a past year or into the coming world is two (it takes the engine about twice the work). A simulate call over both sexes counts the lives of each. A person met is two (born in a past year; a newborn of today is one) for each try a search allows. A live life's start is one (two as someone alive now, or in a past year or the coming world), and each step after it one. A line (/lineage) is two for each generation, with the lives lived again counted in its work. A simulate call with several runs counts each.

Cache what you get. Catalogs carry an ETag (send If-None-Match for a 304) and may be kept for an hour. A call with a seed may be kept for a day; one without is new each time. Every call counts its units, also one answered from the cache, and a call that would go past the month is refused whole, without counting.

Work past the units. Some calls do far more work than their units say: a condition the world rarely has is found by drawing many births, and someone met at an age few reach is born again until one does. Such a call counts the work it did, about one unit for 400 births drawn or 100 years lived, when that is more than half again its units; ordinary calls never count more. usage.this_call says what a call counted. A call that would need more work than its plan allows a call stops with a 400 (too_much_work) and counts the work done; ask for fewer lives or tries, or fewer conditions.

Keys

  • Getting one:The key comes back to the address you wrote from. One key an address.
  • Lost it: write again from the same address. A new key replaces the old one, which stops working; the month's use carries over.
  • Turning it off: write again with “stop” in the subject; the key and its record are deleted.
  • From an AI app (MCP): the app acts with your key, so its calls count as yours, against the plan and the terms. Turning the key off or getting a new one ends its connections.
  • Send it as Authorization: Bearer k8b_…. It is yours alone: keep it private, and do not share or sell it. We keep only its hash, never the key.
  • Keep it on your server (or in your own tools). A key built into a web page or an app people download can be read and copied, and what it is used for counts against you: its units, and the terms. For an app, call the engine from your server and pass on what your users need.
  • Watched, and stopped when misused: for each key we count, for the month, the kinds of calls it makes and the options it uses (above all sensitive, suicide, who a person is, searches), not what you do with the answers. A key that seems to be used against the terms may be turned off without warning, and its address gets no new key.

Errors are JSON too: {"error": "...", "code": "...", "status": 400, "docs": "..."}. 400 a parameter the engine cannot take (or a condition the world does not have), 401 no key or a key it does not know, 404 no such endpoint, 405 a method the endpoint does not take (POST only on /live), 413 a body too large, 429 too many calls or the units used up. code is for programs: bad_request (a parameter), not_in_world (conditions the world does not have, or too rarely, or no one reached the age), too_much_work, plan_size (more than the plan allows a call), token and live_state (a /live token, or a step that does not fit where the life stands), key, rate (calls a minute), month (the month's units; the answer's more links to the plans for more and resets gives the day the month starts again), free_day (the free plan's day), too_many, not_found, method, too_large, unavailable.

Versions

v1 stays as it is: its paths and fields are not removed or renamed; new fields and parameters may appear, and the answers follow the engine. When meta.engine changes, the same seed may give a different life (a live token goes on as it is). The engine changes as LifeSim does, often, and older versions are not served: to reproduce a result, keep meta.engine and the date with it, and the lives you need. A change that would break v1 comes as v2, and v1 keeps working beside it.

Privacy

We keep a count of answers by endpoint, without who asked. A key is kept only as a hash, with its plan, when it was made, the month's units, the month's counts of its calls by kind and option, and the hash of the address it was mailed to (one key an address); it is deleted when it is turned off, and the address hash of a key turned off for misuse is kept so no new key goes to it. A live life's state is in its token, with you, not with us.
