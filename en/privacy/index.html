Privacy policy

LifeSim has no accounts and is built not to ask for names or contact details. This page sets out everything we do handle, why, and where it is kept.

Effective: November 6, 2026

1. What we handle, why, how and for how long

Life records (the life you are living, the list of past lives and the full record of every past life (the latest 10 in the classroom edition), your notes, settings, recent problems on this device (where a script error happened, a save, opening, payment check, memorial, video or move that did not work with a short code for why, the version and the time; 30 days, up to 12), the time crisis words were seen (for 30 days after it, no life on this device ends by or meets a suicide))

Purpose
Continuing a life, reopening past lives, reporting a problem
How it is kept
On your device (browser storage). If you have a keepsake pass, a copy of that browser's records is also kept on our server while "Keep my past lives on the server too" is on (it is on from the start and can be turned off; see "Keeping past lives on the server" below). Recent problems are never sent by themselves; they go along only when you send feedback or write to us with "Report a problem". Part of a life reaches our server only when you choose: when you share it (shared lives) or make a share link (the life’s country, age and sex in the simulation and the four numbers of its grain, or, when you share “The life I wish for” or a life’s grain, its name, are in the link), or move your lives to another browser or phone (see "Moving my lives" below), or put up a memorial or carry a life with your keepsake pass (see the keepsake rows below)
How long
Until you clear the site data in your browser (some browsers, such as Safari, clear data of sites not visited for a while on their own)

Anonymous usage statistics: kinds of screens and actions (a life begins or ends, etc.) with details from the simulation (birth country, the country lived in now after a move abroad, age, auto mode, speed, the world chosen: past, today or future, and for a past world the decade of birth; when a life ends, the "today's theme" of the day it began and whether the life met it), how you arrived (link source, in-app browser type), device type and screen width, country of access (a country code derived from the connection; the address itself is not kept), a random number made fresh for each tab, whether a visit is the first time this browser opens the site

Purpose
Understanding use, finding errors, managing server costs, the "living now" map on the first screen, other people's lanterns in the night sky of the Village of Past Lives
How it is kept
Stored as hourly totals. Whether a visit is a first one is known only from a mark left in this browser (lifesim.seen.v1, with nothing else in it), and the server keeps only whether it was, in hourly totals like the other statistics. It is to tell new visitors from returning ones in understanding how the site is used. The random number and the tab's current state are used only for the "online now" view and change on reload. A visible tab sends its signal every 9 minutes, to cut server requests, and a page left untouched for over 20 minutes sends none. No cookies, advertising IDs or IP addresses are stored. When you send up a lantern in the Village of Past Lives, only its time is kept, in the server's memory for up to 30 minutes, and shown as light alone in other people's night skies; the words, whom it was for and the random number are not kept with it
How long
Totals that cannot identify anyone; the "online now" view (random number included) forgets a tab within 10 minutes of its last signal

Script error reports: version, screen, where the error happened and a short fingerprint (no error text, no address)

Purpose
Fixing errors
How it is kept
Hourly totals
How long
As above

Shared lives (optional): the simulation's story of that life (its name, country, age, sex and the four numbers of its grain), your words (up to 300 characters), language, time posted, "remember" and report counts, a random key for deleting your post (also kept on your device)

Purpose
Sharing lives with others; quoting them, without saying who wrote them, in posts introducing LifeSim (social media and the like)
How it is kept
Public. Contacts, links, anything that looks like a school or an address, and insults are filtered out automatically, but no filter catches everything, so please leave out real names and anything that could identify you. To prevent abuse, the tab's random number is used briefly in server memory only
How long
Deleted at once when you delete it; a post hidden by reports or by us is kept so we can decide whether to restore it, until you delete it or ask us to

Moving my lives (optional): the life records in this browser (the list of past lives, the full record of every past life, the life in progress, your notes and farewells), the numbers and take-down keys of memorials put up from this device, the "country you were really born in" you chose, and whether this browser has a keepsake pass (the pass key is not sent)

Purpose
Moving your lives to another app's browser or a new phone (instead of a login)
How it is kept
Kept briefly on our server under a random code that works once. Nothing that says whose records they are (an account, an email, an IP address) is stored with them. Anyone holding the code can take them, so do not send it to anyone else
How long
Deleted as soon as they are taken, or after 3 days

Keeping past lives on the server (keepsakes, on from the start, can be turned off): the life records of a browser where it is on (what "Moving my lives" carries: the list of past lives, the full record of the lives you can see again, the life in progress, your notes and farewells, the numbers and take-down keys of memorials put up from that device, and the "country you were really born in" you chose)

Purpose
Bringing them back by opening the pass again after the browser's data is cleared, or in another browser
How it is kept
If you have a keepsake pass and "Keep my past lives on the server too" is on in a browser, that browser's records are compressed and kept on our server (Cloudflare). It is on from the start when you buy a pass, and you can turn it off at any time. Only the latest copy of each browser is kept (up to three browsers a pass), under the pass's hash and a random number each browser makes, and it is replaced when a life ends and about every 10 minutes during one. No email or IP address is stored with it. Nothing is sent while it is off
How long
Kept while you have the pass; deleted at once when you turn it off or the pass is refunded (from the copies kept against failures within 30 days, see section 5)

LifeSim Engine API (for developers, optional): the number of calls by kind of answer, and how many of them came from the try page. With a key (given from November 1, 2026): the key's hash, its plan, when it was made, the month's use, and the hash of the email address it was sent to (one key an address), and for each key the month's counts of calls by kind and option (to stop misuse); the address hash of a key turned off for misuse; with a paid plan, its Stripe subscription number (kept with the key) and, until its key is shown, the number of the checkout it was paid at; for a plan bought once from mainland China (30 days or a year), the payment number, the hash of the address paid with, the plan, its days and when it was bought; for a plan given free for public-interest use, the address hash and the day it ends; with an AI app connected (MCP), the hashes of that connection's sign-in code and tokens (with the key's hash, the hash of the app's id and the address the app goes back to)

Purpose
Giving and checking API keys, preventing abuse and keeping server costs in check, checking a key's monthly limit, starting and ending paid plans, giving plans free for public-interest use, connecting AI apps (MCP)
How it is kept
Calls are stored only as hourly totals, without who made them.Our server (Cloudflare) keeps the key and the email address only as irreversible hashes, never the key itself, the address or a name. How many calls may be made in a minute is counted by Cloudflare for that minute only; the operator stores nothing of it. For each key we also count, for the month, how many calls of each kind and with which options (lines the site keeps on the device, conditions about who a person is, and so on) it made, so the operator can turn off a key that seems to be used against the terms; an address whose key was turned off for that gets no new key. Totals are kept in a form that identifies no one · a key's record is deleted at once when the key is turned off (by writing again with "stop" in the subject, or on request; from the copies kept against failures within 30 days, see section 5) · the address hash of a key turned off for misuse is kept, so no new key is sent to it · a paid plan's subscription number while the subscription runs; when it ends, the key goes back to the free plan and the number is deleted · a checkout's number 3 days after paying · a plan bought once from mainland China when its time ends (at once if refunded) · a free public-interest plan's record when it ends (after a year) or when its key is turned off · an AI app connection's sign-in code within 5 minutes, its access token after an hour, its refresh token when used or after 90 days, and all of a key's connections when the key is turned off or replaced

4. Children and teenagers

We do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 14 (16 in some countries). Please do not write names, contact details, schools or where you live in shared lives or feedback; we try to filter such details out automatically, though no filter catches everything, and if we learn that a child's personal information has reached us, we delete it. Under the terms of Link, the seller, the keepsake pass must be paid for by an adult; minors should ask a parent or guardian to pay.

For parents and guardians. LifeSim is a life simulation made for ages 15 and up. It lives one person's life by real statistics, so death, illness, war and poverty, drinking, smoking, gambling, prison, violence at home, early marriage and births in the teens happen as they do in reality, and a past world tells of real history such as wars and mass killings. Drug dependence in adult lives, and sexual violence from age 15, also happen as often as the real statistics say, never shown in detail or how, always with where to find help, and never on shared cards or memorials (suicide happens from the teenage years on, as often as the real statistics say, never showing how or why, never named on shared cards or memorials, and always with where to find help). Players cannot talk to or contact each other, there are no ads, and paying never changes a life in the simulation.

Classroom mode. Teachers who want to use it in class can open classroom mode from 'Open in classroom mode' at the bottom of the first screen. A browser opened that way holds back content by our own guideline of ages 12 and up (no suicide, no violence or physical punishment at home, no drug dependence or sexual violence, no assisted dying in the family, no choosing to drink, smoke, gamble or commit a crime, and only today's world), and turns off shared lives, feedback, problem reports, moving records, keepsakes and memorials. So in classroom mode no names, contact details or pupils' words reach our server, and what is written in the pauses and parting words stays on the device. Only anonymous statistics that identify no one are sent, and not even those if the browser has "Do Not Track" or Global Privacy Control turned on. That classroom mode is on is kept in that browser only.

5. Deletion

Information is deleted without delay once its period ends or its purpose is met, in a way that cannot be recovered.

Copies against failures. So that nothing is lost to a failure or a mistake, the server's records (keepsake payments and passes, memorials, shared lives, anonymous statistics and operating records) have copies. Cloudflare, which runs the server, keeps 30 days of changes so the server can go back to a point within them; once a day we copy the records to storage inside the same Cloudflare account and keep each copy for 14 days; and we may download a copy from the admin page and keep it encrypted (deleted after 30 days). So a post or memorial you delete or take down is gone from the site and the server at once, and from the copies within 30 days. Copies are used only to bring back records lost to a failure or a mistake.

6. Your rights

  • We reply within 10 days.
  • You can delete a shared life yourself from the device you posted it on. A memorial can be taken down from the device that made it. What you delete is gone at once (from the copies kept against failures within 30 days, see section 5).
  • Life records on your device disappear when you clear the site data in your browser.
  • Anonymous statistics are totals that cannot identify anyone, so one person's part cannot be singled out.
  • You may also complain to your data protection authority.
  • If you are in Brazil, you have the rights of Brazil's General Data Protection Law (LGPD), exercised the same way: confirmation that we process your data, access, correction, anonymization or deletion, portability, information on whom it is shared with, and withdrawing consent. You may also complain to Brazil's data protection authority (ANPD).

7. Cookies and similar technology

We set no cookies (Cloudflare, which delivers the site, may set a strictly necessary security cookie, and the checkout uses cookies under Stripe's policy). Life records and settings are kept in browser storage (localStorage, and IndexedDB for the full records of past lives with a keepsake pass) and leave your device only when you share or carry a life, as set out in section 1. While "Keep my past lives on the server too" is on in the keepsakes (it is from the start), a copy of that browser's records is also kept on our server (section 1). You can block or clear site data in your browser settings; continuing a life and past lives then will not work.

8. Security

All connections are encrypted (HTTPS), we handle only what is needed, IP addresses are not stored, the admin page needs a secret key, and a content security policy blocks outside scripts. Our server handles email addresses only as hashes.
